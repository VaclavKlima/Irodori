# General object selection and manual refinement

## Outcome and scope — November 8, 2026

**Subsequent implementation:** [Object selection](object-selection.md) is now enabled on Mac. The user explicitly approved delivery on the available device and [Bazzite follow-up](README.md#task-69); that supersedes the research recommendation to delay activation until Radeon qualification. The evidence and unqualified photographic/cross-platform claims below remain historical research findings.

The bounded feasibility study for [TODO 54](README.md#task-54), shared with [TODO 66](README.md#task-66), establishes local native-GPU inference and a model-independent manual-correction representation. It does **not** activate automatic masks in the Editor or Console. Both backlog items remain open. Production dependencies, settings/catalog versions, processing algorithms and the user's managed library were unchanged by this research.

The intended tool selects ordinary user-indicated objects: animals, people, plants, vehicles, products and other visible objects. Birds are one acceptance subject. A reflection is a separate indicated region or object; it is not automatically inferred from a subject silhouette. Initial selection should be useful and editable. Automatic hair/fur/feather matting, sky classification, physical transparency and new local color/Texture/Clarity/dehaze operators remain separate work.

## Frozen study

The [protocol](../../assets/tests/references/object-selection/protocol.json), [twelve-scene manifest](../../assets/tests/references/object-selection/inputs.json) and [reproduction tools](../../scripts/research/object-selection/README.md) retain source hashes, exact upstream revisions, prompts and execution rules. There are nine development scenes and three held-out scenes; alternate exposures do not count as additional scenes. Existing permitted fixtures cover dog fur, adjacent boats/reflection, low-contrast trunks, an oriented Sony RAW fence, paper and shoreline pine. Six independently privacy-reviewed, licensed, unchanged [new originals](../../assets/tests/images/raster/wikimedia/object-selection/README.md) add a wig, bicycle, adjacent flamingos, plastic mug, robin and glass mug. Their bytes and provenance were retained in the fixture-only local commit `b5db9fc5c5c16ef834b82dab11c1a36453d33a36` before the final measurements.

The research image is the full-native, once-oriented neutral SDR source without crop, lens or local edits. Sony RAW uses the application's sensor decoder and GPU preparation, not a camera embedded JPEG. This deliberately stable source is independent of zoom, pan and tonal edits. It is not yet production crop/lens prompt registration. Native source preparation took approximately 3.7–4.0 seconds for the tested 20 MP RAWs and 0.3–1.3 seconds for the new raster inputs; preprocessing, inference, reconstruction, IPC and presentation are distinct costs.

Every scene uses a frozen box, then the same box plus one positive and one or two negative points. Box corners consume two prompt slots. The six-slot comparator limit is enforced by refusing overflow. No prompt was moved in response to model output. Both candidates select the highest returned predicted-IoU alternative, first on ties, then bilinearly reconstruct 256 × 256 logits to the complete native grid with `align_corners=false` and threshold zero. Scores rank the model's alternatives; they are not measured accuracy, semantic approval, opacity or confidence suitable for automatic acceptance.

The +1 EV views double linear-sRGB values inside binary coverage and clamp SDR for diagnostic review. These CPU-generated research artifacts are not application RAW/HDR edits or export acceptance. The corpus covers raster and Sony RAW inputs; authored gain-map HDR and merge-source selection remain unqualified. The isolated wig covers hair-like boundaries only; real human-portrait hair remains unverified. Glass and translucent plastic provide semantic stress cases without controlled alpha ground truth. The held-out robin's original main-body box excludes part of its left-clipped tail; that prompt limitation was retained rather than adjusted after freezing.

## Native runtime and numerical findings

The tested backend is **ONNX Runtime Node 1.31.1 native WebGPU**, through the application's bundled Node 24.20.0 on Apple M4 Pro/macOS arm64. Runtime installation is isolated under ignored research artifacts; root npm dependencies and lockfile are unchanged. The public Node binding exposes the native provider but not adapter identity or GPU peak memory. These results establish that provider on this host; Metal is its documented macOS path. They do not establish Linux/Radeon support or physical GPU allocation peaks. See the [official versioned platform table](https://github.com/microsoft/onnxruntime/blob/v1.31.1/js/node/README.md) and [native WebGPU provider documentation](https://onnxruntime.ai/docs/execution-providers/WebGPU-ExecutionProvider.html).

The small known numeric graph passed eleven executions. A deliberately unsupported CPU-only operator was refused before inference, with CPU fallback disabled. Synthetic and actual-model cancellation killed the owned child, left no published completed result and confirmed the PID was gone. Actual-model cancellation occurred after encoding/features existed; this proves ownership and publication fencing at that phase, not cooperative production cancellation or measured GPU-memory drain.

Original int64-label exports encoded on GPU but their decoders were explicitly refused for unsupported integer prompt-label operators. Those failures are preserved. One bounded compatibility adjustment changes only categorical input labels to float32, validating exact values `-1, 0, 1, 2, 3`. Its fixed synthetic decoder-probe outputs were bitwise identical to the original int64 PyTorch computation. The repaired FP32 graphs then ran with CPU fallback disabled and native profiles containing only `WebGpuExecutionProvider`. Original upstream int64-label PyTorch outputs remain independent numerical references, explicitly diagnostic CPU work rather than a fallback. The fixed comparison tolerance is `1e-3 + 1e-3 × abs(reference)`; no numerical gates were relaxed.

Pinned candidate weights are 156,008,466 bytes for SAM 2.1 Hiera Tiny and 40,982,470 bytes for EfficientSAM ViT-T. Both upstream sources are Apache-2.0; exact commits, checkpoint SHA-256 values, original licenses and exported graph hashes are retained in the [model manifest](../../scripts/research/object-selection/model-sources.json) and local export receipts. A production download/package policy is still required. See [SAM 2](https://github.com/facebookresearch/sam2) and [EfficientSAM](https://github.com/yformer/EfficientSAM).

The complete nine-development run passed for both candidates: 36 output tensors and 3,538,998 scalar output values per model, zero out-of-tolerance samples. EfficientSAM had identical low-resolution logit signs. SAM 2 had one near-zero sign change among the wig box-stage logits (GPU `+3.78e-7`, reference `-7.78e-7`), in an unselected candidate; every other low-resolution sign matched. Maximum absolute errors were approximately `2.43e-4` for EfficientSAM and `8.20e-5` for SAM 2. This is numerical agreement within the frozen gate, not bit-exact GPU inference. Low-resolution sign agreement also does not establish byte-identical native masks after interpolation.

## Manual correction and persistence reference

The research [manual-refinement prototype](../../scripts/research/object-selection/manual-refinement.py) keeps an immutable, content-addressed lossless native R8 PNG base separate from normalized Paint/Erase polylines. Paint combines with `max`; Erase multiplies existing coverage by `1 - brushCoverage`; smoothstep supplies feathering; whole-mask inversion applies last. Replacing the AI base on the same frozen source/grid preserves the ordered manual strokes. Source/grid changes and missing or tampered content are refused. No model is needed for reload, history navigation or coverage rendering.

Six checks pass against independent scalar and tile-seam references, including ordering/inversion, byte-exact reload, same-grid base replacement, Undo/Redo and stale/tampered input rejection. The synthetic 5472 × 3648 reference uses 330 tiles with at most 256 × 256 pixels, a maximum floating tile plane of 262,144 bytes, about 2.18 MB of traced temporary allocations and 85.6 MB process peak RSS; one coverage render took 0.252 seconds. Those are CPU research-reference measurements. They do not prove production GPU coverage, database transactions, preview/export history or brush UI latency. At this size, the decoded R8 base and output each occupy 19,961,856 bytes (39,923,712 bytes together); the floating-tile figure is not total process scratch. Traced allocation peak excludes some native/Pillow allocations, which the process RSS includes. Native R8 storage is one byte per source pixel before PNG compression; it must be included in future admission and retained-history budgets.

A second check uses the actual saved SAM 2 dog coverage. [Two fixed illustrative strokes](../../assets/tests/references/object-selection/manual-dog-strokes.json) paint a missing interior fur patch and erase a selected ground island. Seven checks verify unchanged original AI assets, byte-exact model-free reload, retained stroke identities and sampled corrections after replacing the base with the saved box-stage result, Undo/Redo and changed-grid refusal. The [evidence](../../artifacts/research/object-selection/2026-11-08/manual-real-dog-demo/demonstration-evidence.json) and [native comparison](../../artifacts/research/object-selection/2026-11-08/manual-real-dog-demo/scripted-correction-native-panel.png) remain local artifacts. Exactly two scripted strokes demonstrate the representation; they do not measure full-mask correction effort or an implemented application workflow.

## Recommended application implementation

1. **Qualify the common backend first.** Run the frozen exports, parity checks, refusal/cancellation tests and photographic cohort on Bazzite's available Radeon adapters. Keep a single native WebGPU inference implementation for Mac and Radeon, selected before execution. Unsupported runtime/device/model conditions report their real reason and preserve existing masks. GPU failure offers a safe retry; it never starts equivalent heavy CPU inference. A failed Radeon gate blocks production adoption rather than introducing an unapproved platform-specific backend.
2. **Add a main-owned selection service.** Use one bounded worker and typed prepare/refine/cancel/preview/accept operations through the shared photo API. Requests identify photo, confirmed source, expected revision and authoritative canonical geometry; renderer/MCP callers never supply filesystem paths, model code or arbitrary prompts to an external command. Initially retain the qualified six-slot prompt limit with explicit overflow refusal; expanded/dynamic prompts and nonzero prior-mask refinement require new export/reference/runtime qualification. Cache one source embedding under explicit admission/lifetime rules and dispose it on source change, cancellation, deletion and shutdown. Keep image preparation and feature reuse separate from expensive inference.
3. **Extend the existing durable mask representation.** Store a lossless R8 generated base and ordered existing brush corrections as separate immutable assets. Version and migrate current/history/frozen-export descriptors explicitly; commit assets and history atomically, protect undo/redo/export owners, and fail clearly for missing content. Replacing a proposal must preserve manual corrections and offer an explicit undoable action. Name, enabled/invert state, order and six existing Light controls use the current multiple-mask core. Reload, Undo/Redo and accepted exports must not rerun a model.
4. **Register coordinates once.** Inference sees the complete once-oriented canonical source. Convert click/box prompts from committed presentation geometry through the existing crop/lens mapping into that source; never use requested zoom/pan or provisional catalog dimensions. Associate cached features and proposals with source/grid identities, including equal-sized rotations. Nonlinear lens-mapped display boxes need an explicit region/polygon mapping contract rather than a guessed affine rectangle. Accepted native coverage is sampled through the same canonical mapping in SDR/HDR preview, histograms and frozen output.
5. **Present a proposal users can repair.** Add a compact Select object tool using the approved Sunflower controls, mutually exclusive with crop, brush and pickers. A click or box starts a proposal; positive/negative points refine it. Show its coverage and retain prior accepted coverage while work is pending. Paint/Erase, size, feather, enable/invert, history and keyboard access remain available through the existing mask editor. An inferior refinement can be undone; the last model score never commits a selection automatically. Escape, blur, lost capture and photo/source change cancel unfinished input while preserving committed edits. Preserve middle-button panning, independent inspector scrolling and 1100 × 700 Console-open usability.
6. **Give Console the same operation.** Freeze photo/source/grid, explicit target context and expected revisions after pending edits flush. The Console can request an indicated object or refine it with points through the same service, inspect the proposed mask/region, accept it and append manual strokes. Describe concrete indicated objects; text grounding or automatic identification of arbitrary named objects is not supplied by these point/box models and needs its own design if desired. Subsequent navigation cannot retarget a turn. Preserve expected-revision conflicts and shared Undo/Redo.

Useful editable semantic coverage is the first delivery. Fine-edge matting, human-portrait quality, automatic subject discovery, sky/background policy, reflection correspondence, feathering policy for generated hard edges, local color and additional local operators need explicit follow-ups. The current fixed-threshold masks do not justify halo-free feather/hair acceptance.

## Verification and remaining acceptance

The explicit `object-selection-research` target registers six fast evidence-gate cases: GPU smoke alone is insufficient; CPU/unproven assignment blocks qualification; repeated scenes/bytes cannot pad the cohort; private/untracked/unreviewed/unlicensed sources do not count; held-outs/categories are required; and successful research qualifies implementation planning only. [Focused research/registry checks](../../artifacts/verification/2026-11-08T13-52-45.019Z-check-gAv56W/summary.md) pass **30 cases in 19.62 seconds** (six research gates plus the verification-registry suite), with type checking, lint and formatting. The six-check manual reference and seven-check scripted photographic demonstration have their own research receipts; they are not application integration tests. Existing application/UI/GPU checks were not repeated because no production behavior changed in this research.

The final [research evidence and qualification result](../../artifacts/research/object-selection/2026-11-08/summary.md) explicitly remain incomplete for production/model adoption: Linux/Radeon runtime evidence and native fine-edge photographic quality are unqualified. Completing the research implementation does not mark those gates as passed.

No comprehensive checkpoint, Bazzite/Windows qualification, production model packaging, complete source-to-coverage/UI timing, real user correction timing, annotated photographic accuracy or physical alpha measurement was performed. The committed protocol does not turn missing evidence into a pass. TODO 54's production subject selection, refinement, history/restart/export and fine-edge acceptance remain open; TODO 66 shares that future service rather than adding another inference backend.
