# Default scene-to-display rendering

Supported Sony RAWs and merge masters use **linear preparation → white balance and Light edits → ACES 2 → canvas encoding**. Neutral settings preserve capture exposure and As Shot white balance. There is one automatic rendering model, with the existing SDR comparison. JPEG, PNG and other raster inputs are already display-referred: their color-managed pixels bypass the scene-rendering transform.

## Capture units

RAW preparation subtracts documented black levels and scales against nominal sensor saturation. LibRaw's automatic brightness and observed-maximum adjustment are disabled. Camera gains are applied once; restoration of the decoder's normalized gains precedes camera-color conversion into linear Rec.2020/D65. Input exposure scale and `referenceWhite` are fixed at **1**, independent of image contents. Dark scenes receive no histogram-based brightness adjustment. Signed channels and above-white highlights remain in Float32 working assets. Existing sensor-saturation blending, lens correction and orientation keep their documented preparation order.

The LibRaw camera matrix and the `max(gains)/min(gains)` exposure scale are approximations. They have not been measured against a Sony color chart or an absolute scene-exposure calibration. Nominal sensor saturation is not a measured scene white. Saturation metadata describes sensor sites separately from display clipping.

## Reference algorithm and units

The complete forward [ACES 2 core](https://github.com/aces-aswf/aces-core/tree/069b0bc3e1f6c62820f19fdae2fecec3f4fc0f80) is pinned at `069b0bc3e1f6c62820f19fdae2fecec3f4fc0f80`. The unmodified CTL and Apache-2.0 license live in [third_party/aces-core](../third_party/aces-core/README.md). The CPU and WGSL ports include the prescribed AP1 input limiting, JMh conversion, tonescale, chroma compression, hue-dependent reach/cusp/upper-hull tables, gamut compression and limiting-primary conversion. Reference white limiting follows the transform. No fitted photographic look replaces these stages.

Rec.2020/D65 converts through XYZ and Von Kries Bradford adaptation to ACES2065-1/AP0/D60. HDR then uses the fixed official **Rec.2020/D65, 1000-nit ACES 2 preset**, with a 100-nit rendering reference: relative peak **10×**, approximately **+3.32 stops**. The versioned `hdr-content-v1-aces2-rec2020-1000-d65` descriptor records these software units. They do not measure panel luminance. White balance and Light edits precede this rendition, which is independent of the attached monitor.

HDR presentation is a reference proof of that content: convert linear Rec.2020 into supported P3 or sRGB canvas primaries, clamp channels to the current presentation limit, then apply extended sRGB encoding once into `rgba16float` in extended mode. P3 requires both canvas support and reported display gamut. Chromium performs final display color management. Limited headroom does not reshape the content or stretch it to the display maximum. Hard clipping may change unavailable highlights or colors. SDR preview, CPU fallback and on-demand proofs retain the direct SDR ACES rendition from the adjusted working source; they keep the HDR content histogram available. UI brightness stays SDR.

Reported headroom and gamut are separate capabilities. `headroom = peak luminance / reference-white luminance` and `stops = log2(headroom)`; these relative values do not establish absolute nits. Unknown headroom remains unavailable. Diagnostics distinguish content luminance exceeding reported headroom, canvas channel clipping, gamut limitation and RAW sensor saturation.

Fixed HDR and SDR tables prepare once in a dedicated renderer worker. Monitor updates change only lightweight presentation mapping and diagnostics. The latest completed photograph remains visible during same-photo edits and target updates. Before uses neutral Light edits and As Shot white balance. `working-hdr` statistics remain adjusted scene-linear Rec.2020, `content-hdr` describes the fixed Rec.2020 rendition, and `output` describes the actual displayed HDR proof or direct SDR rendition. Pixel inspection uses these same domain mappings. Overlays and the pixel grid never alter measurements.

## Interactive presentation

The GPU evaluates white balance, Light edits and ACES over needed source texels. A bounded LRU retains **Float32 canonical HDR content tiles** and direct SDR variants. Pan and zoom sample the cache, map presentation, encode once and draw overlays; the presentation fragment shader does not evaluate ACES. HDR headroom and gamut changes reuse content tiles. Edits retire After tiles across variants; neutral Before survives and shares neutral After tiles.

Production tiles have 64 × 64 interiors and a one-pixel halo. Smaller sources use smaller tiles. At most 2048 layers share the existing **768 MiB** total budget, capped by adapter limits and reduced for source, analysis and Retina surfaces. HDR/SDR and Before/After variants use this same cache. Memory accounting includes the offscreen completed-frame surface, a bounded 1024-pixel retained surface for device replacement, and the active photo's two histogram sample resolutions. Each completed frame updates the retained copy before its WebGPU drawing buffer expires; device-loss recovery never tries to read an already lost buffer. The copy is temporary SDR transport, carries the completed presentation serial, and never enters statistics. CPU fallback reuses matching content bins while recalculating current display warnings, avoiding backend rounding changes at bin boundaries. Upload buffers are released before allocating the cache. Disposal releases textures, buffers and workers.

One immutable GPU request runs at a time, with one replaceable pending request. Refinement computes at most eight tiles per batch, checks newer input after GPU completion and retires unfinished slots. Completed drafts advance monotonically during dragging; cancelled gestures, history replacement and display epochs reject obsolete output. The offscreen surface replaces the canvas only after all its work completes. Commits retain validated source identity and do not upload the source again.

During edits, adaptive scene-linear mips bound the visible source footprint to approximately 8192 texels, preserving CSS zoom and framing. Release restores normal detail. CPU fallback first renders a bounded 1024-pixel sensor-derived draft while its full-resolution SDR refinement continues; it retains the previous completed pixels during that work. At normal 100% and above, one decoded image pixel remains one CSS pixel and magnification uses nearest source pixels. Reduction renders a chosen scene-linear mip and interpolates premultiplied display-linear RGBA before encoding once. Photo-wide analysis is independent of framing: stable GPU samples number 8192 while dragging and 65536 after release. Pan/zoom reuse analysis. Expensive exact masks run separately; explicit committed statistics still scan the full validated source.

The RGB content histogram always has 256 encoded SDR bins plus 256 log2 bins through **+4 stops**. Monitor updates move only its capability marker and warnings. Controls, readouts and detail rows stay mounted while results arrive. Curves retarget an 80 ms animation starting on the next animation frame and respect reduced motion.

## Automatic cutover

Settings v7 and catalog schema 12 rewrite processing metadata in every history snapshot transactionally. RAWs and merge masters adopt `hdr-v1`; raster photos adopt `display-referred-v1`. Cursor, revisions, timestamps, redo branches and numeric edits remain intact. There is no per-photo upgrade operation or previous-look renderer.

`hdr-source-v2-capture-scale-1` (with the existing `sensor-blend-v1` suffix for RAWs), `hdr-output-v6-content-proof-aces2-069b0bc3-v1` and `hdr-analysis-v4-content-aces2-069b0bc3-v1` retire derived output and analysis. Preview cache v9 and the existing source identities retain prepared working assets. Originals, merge masters and numeric edit history remain unchanged. Originals are byte-preserved. Existing composite masters, including descriptors carrying `hdr-source-v1`, remain readable without rewriting their bytes; only their derived rendition changes. Retired Natural code, reference photographs and prior measurements are preserved locally under ignored `artifacts/natural-reference/` as research evidence, with no Lightroom mismatch gate.

## Verification and limits

`display-rendering` checks CPU results against independent golden vectors. `display-rendering-reference` recompiles the unmodified pinned CTL through a separate C++ Float64 adapter and checks actual hardware WGSL. The adapter does not import the generated application implementation. Synthetic vectors cover dense neutral/color ramps, signed channels, saturation, alpha, headrooms 1, 2, 4 and 8 in both canvas gamuts, and fixed Rec.2020 at peak 10. `hdr-math-gpu` adds exposure doubling, white balance and extreme combined Light edits. The retained per-channel rendering tolerance is `2e-6 + 2e-5 × abs(reference)`; transfer encoding is checked independently at that tolerance using measured linear inputs. WGSL retains compensated cone responses, angular conversions and gamut intersections with explicit rounding boundaries. Compensated Horner evaluation replaces repeated divisions in logarithm/exponential series, and selective native powers retain the same reference tolerance.

`hdr-content` and `hdr-content-gpu` check monitor-independent bins, real beyond-headroom content, colored channel clipping, primary conversion and single encoding through CPU and actual GPU paths. Native GPU SDR proof generation uses the existing bounded strip converter with a CPU fallback; its test checks RGB quantization against CPU ACES, exact alpha, hashes and unchanged working bytes. `hdr-render-cache` checks reuse, invalidation, bounded batching and comparison sharing. `hdr-render-cache-gpu` verifies actual tile computation against the independent CTL at the same tolerance, including halos, signed colors, alpha and both gamuts at headrooms 1, 2, 4 and 8. It independently checks premultiplied interpolation and single encoding using measured cached texels. `hdr-interaction-ui` checks native framing, edits, display changes and console layout. `hdr-interaction-benchmark` waits for actual GPU completion after pan, wheel zoom and mouse-drag updates; submission alone cannot establish responsiveness. The complete diagonal mouse path is prepared before its five warmups and 40 retained samples, so intermediate corners are cached with the smaller tiles. Its warmed p95 limit is 33 ms. `hdr-content-ui` checks source retention, history, histogram geometry and visibility, including sampled Electron compositor captures for blank frames. `hdr-editor-ui` checks nonempty retained pixels during device loss. `hdr-slider-benchmark` measures all eight enabled sliders at Fit and 100%, four input events per measured burst, plus uninterrupted input every animation frame, actual GPU completion and the following presentation frame. Histogram timing includes the first curve update, not just completed bins. Continuous cases verify monotonic completed serials, matching histograms and one history entry per gesture. Frame and histogram p95 limits are 33 ms; release-to-normal-detail must stay within 500 ms. This evidence excludes physical input delivery and scanout.

Reference conformance establishes software behavior. Photographic review is recorded separately. Absolute reconstruction of the observed scene, measured camera characterization, physical display luminance and broader platform certification remain unverified. ACES is an engineering choice for a consistent, perceptually useful rendition; HDR standards do not uniquely reconstruct the appearance of a past scene to its observer.
